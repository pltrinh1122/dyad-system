# Problem discovery: evaluation

Segment: **evaluation**, a way of working with AI agents, not a job. The person judges what an agent produced (code,
an analysis, a document, an answer) before relying on it or passing it on. Readers: anyone whose work includes judging
agent output, from people using a chat window out of the box to people running agents that act.

Procedure: the chronicle craft's `crafts/chronicle/rules/collateral.md` §6, Steps 1–2. Derived independently: no other
segment's tree, canvas or the projection was read. Every statement here is a hypothesis (H); none is judged yet.

## Step 1. Anchor

Root anchor, as briefed: **"I answer for the result"**: the person is held to what they rely on or pass on.

Written for the segment:

> When I let an agent **produce code, an analysis, a document or an answer** that **I rely on or pass on under my
> name**, I want to trust it enough to answer for the result, so that **what I put my name to holds up when someone
> leans on it, and I can say why I believed it.**

## Step 2. Diverge, by lens

Unjudged, at least two per lens; a statement may fit more than one lens and is listed once.

**Visibility** (can I see what it did)
- I see the answer, not what it looked at or skipped to get there.
- I can't tell whether it actually ran, read or checked anything, or only wrote as if it had.
- When it changed many things at once, I can't see which change was the one that mattered.

**Verifiability** (can I check it is right)
- The only way I have to check it is to do the work again myself.
- It names a source, a figure or a rule, and when I look, it isn't there or doesn't say that.
- I can't tell whether a fact is out of date, true somewhere else, or invented.

**Predictability** (will it do what it said)
- It says it did something it didn't do, or did only part of it.
- The same request gives me a different answer the next time, so one good check doesn't carry over.
- It is careful on one task and careless on the next, with nothing to warn me which this is.

**Reversibility** (can I undo it)
- Once I've passed it on, I can't take it back; others have already built on it.
- When it turns out wrong, I can't tell what else I sent that came from the same run.

**Attribution** (can I show who decided)
- When asked why something is in there, I can't say whether I chose it or the agent did.
- "The agent said so" is no answer to give anyone who holds me to it.
- I can't point to what it was told, so I can't show the mistake wasn't mine.

**Competence** (does it know this domain)
- It writes just as fluently about things it gets wrong as about things it gets right.
- In a field I don't know well, I can't tell a good answer from a plausible one.
- It misses the local rule everyone here knows but nobody wrote down.

**Intent** (did it understand what I meant)
- It answered a slightly different question than the one I meant, and the answer looked fine.
- What I asked left room for more than one reading, and it picked one without saying so.
- It wrote for an expert when I needed something I could hand to someone who isn't one, or the other way round.

**Attention** (do I have time to check)
- Checking it takes about as long as doing it myself, sometimes longer.
- It gives me far more than I can read with care, faster than I can read it.
- I can't tell which parts need checking, so I check all of it, or give up and check none.
- After the tenth good answer I stop checking the eleventh.

**Consequence** (what breaks if it is wrong)
- A small wrong detail can be the one someone else acts on.
- I don't know how much rides on each part, so I can't put my checking where a mistake would cost most.

**Accountability to others** (who will hold me to it)
- The person I hand it to assumes I checked it.
- If it is wrong, it is my mistake now, not the agent's.
- I get asked to explain something I can't follow myself.

**Other**
- I feel I'm slower than people who don't check, and wonder whether checking is worth it.
