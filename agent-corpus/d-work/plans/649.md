## Intent as read: the intent reading (`intent-reading: on`, Rule-3 Plan)
Prompt: provenance #649, entry 1.

**What decides today, and why it is too coarse.** `dmc:precedent-admission`'s `operator-side` outcome refuses every case
of a record if any outcome's consequence matches one regular expression of words: ratify, dispose, plan-Y, done-Y,
merge, release, destructive, archive (`dyad/guards/agent/dmc.py` `RATIFYING`). It is a word test on the whole record:
- release-class is refused because its outcomes say "released with the change set". Yet its class only fills in the
  number a release question shows you, and you answer that question anyway.
- plan-departure is refused whole, though its `new plan` outcome only asks you a question.

**What a precedent must never do (Rule-2).** It must never remove or replace a disposition. A wrong match is safe when
it costs at most a question you answer, and unsafe when it skips a question or acts.

**The record proposed: `dmc:precedent-scope`.**
- **Kind:** mechanical, over a declared field.
- **Home:** `rules/RULE-2-no-self-ratify.md`, because the line it draws is that a precedent never disposes.
- **alsoIn:** Rule-3 (What a `Y` binds) and `scripts/similar.py`.
- **Decides:** may a precedent settle this outcome?
- **Input:** the outcome's `dyad:effect`, which each record declares per outcome. The declaration is reviewed in the
  PR that adds it.
- **Outcomes:**

| effect | when (the outcome's consequence) | may a precedent settle it? |
|---|---|---|
| `settles` | an agent-side call: no question is asked or skipped, and nothing is ratified (changelog-actor, promotion, attack-result today) | yes |
| `asks` | it puts a question to you: a new plan, a plan revision, a row of its own, a preferences PR you dispose | yes: a wrong match costs one question, and you still decide |
| `shapes` | it fills in what a question shows you that you answer anyway, such as a release class, which sets the number in the release question | yes: you see it in the question |
| `skips` | it means a question is **not** asked that another outcome would ask (execution detail, as planned, carried, ride, port) | **no**: a precedent abstains, and the call stays inference |
| `acts` | it performs a ratification, a destructive act, a release or a preference change with no question of its own | **no**, and no record admitted today has one |

- **Precedence:** `acts` over `skips` over the rest. An outcome with no declared effect counts as `skips`, so it is
  refused until declared.
- **Consequences in code:**
  - `similar.decide` never returns a `skips` or `acts` outcome. When the nearest precedent holds one, it abstains
    with the reason `scope`.
  - `similar.admit`'s `operator-side` refusal becomes per case: a case whose outcome is `skips` or `acts` is refused.
  - The dmc guard requires `dyad:effect` on each outcome of a record of kind `precedent`, and keeps `RATIFYING` only
    as a warning where a declared `settles`, `asks` or `shapes` outcome holds those words.

**Applied to the 20 refused entries:**
- blocker-home 4: own-diff `settles`, own-row and revision ×2 `asks` → **4 admitted**.
- plan-departure 4: new-plan ×2 `asks` → **2 admitted**; execution-detail ×2 `skips` → **2 stay refused**.
- release-class 6: capability ×3, enhancement ×2 and critical-fix all `shapes` → **6 admitted**.
- unit-lifecycle 6: idle-unit and keep ×2 `settles`; re-seat and retire ×2 `asks` (a preferences PR, or the unit's own

So 9 + 18 = 27 usable, and 2 stay refused. Agreement is then measured per record, and none converts until it meets
its bar.
