## Terms used in the attacks
- **Sub-system entity:** a part with its *own* components, state, interfaces and lifecycle,
  deployable and failing on its own.
- **Mode:** a way of operating that is chosen by a parameter, over components shared with the other
  modes.

The claim splits into **C1** "they are modes" and **C2** "they are not sub-systems", and each is
attacked separately.

| # | attack | result | evidence |

## Verdict
- **C1 ("they are modes") survives, re-scoped:** they are modes of **an act**, not of the
  architecture (A3, A5).
- **C2 ("not sub-system entities") survives** for explicit and implicit (A1, A6, A7), and **is
  refuted for automatic's realization** (A1, A2). Automatic mode *requires* one mode-exclusive
  sub-system, the non-inferring executor, while the mode itself stays an attribute of the act.

## Survivor, as the craft would state it
> **Mode** — the way one act obtains its countersignature and who processes it: **explicit**
> (countersigned live by a human; an agent processes), **implicit** (countersigned in advance by a
> standing mandate; an agent processes), **automatic** (countersigned beforehand by a countersigned
> release; a non-inferring executor processes). Every act has exactly one mode at a time and changes
> mode only along an edge: **delegate** (explicit → implicit), **codify** (explicit or implicit →
> automatic), **escalate** (automatic or implicit → explicit). Modes are not sub-systems.
> **Sub-systems** — the agent runtime (explicit and implicit), the **executor** (automatic only; the
> one mode-exclusive sub-system), and the ledger, the guards, the countersign record and the
> intake (all modes).

**Checkable consequence:** every act record carries `mode` and a reference to its
countersignature. A guard can then enforce the survivor: exactly one mode per act, transitions only
along the named edges, and an automatic act's processor never an agent. The concept thereby becomes
a validator (dsys's C5 test: "validator-writable") rather than prose.
