## Intent reading (revision 1)

Prompt (entry 1): "i need a strategy for helping me manage my attention across units, board and backlog.  propose based on industry best practices.  i'm a human with whims of the day and so things that matters to me more now may not have as much emphasis later so your strategy and playbook must account for my shift in "mood".  having said that, i will be influenced when grounded on intermediate and long-term goals/intent as those don't shift daily.  your playbook needs to account for this operator preference.  other operators will have their own so contain the personalized rules and `dmc` while the strategy and mechanism should remain the same.  the home unit's primary mandate is to manage operator's attention while delegating all other work, including this specific d-work from this prompt."

Read against: `dyad dwork intent 705` (top: #700, #290, #670, #367, #703, #620); `dmc:promotion` (classes: a live defect, a promise, a fired trigger, adjacent to the last delivered row, worked under backlog, the oldest valid row); `dmc:recommended-next` (re-present, audit, groom, hardening, promote); the backlog-groom play-book and `dmc:backlog-staleness` (#697); the closing line's Awaiting / Coming / Recommended parts (Rule-3 Batch disposition); the attention ledger (#700, #703); the cabinet Guide §1 ("the home holds your attention, not authority"), §8 and §12 (`dmc/attention-problem.jsonld`); the program charter in `preferences-corpus/MANDATES.md`; `dmc:preference-kind`.

The proposed synthesis is three horizons (long-term intent, intermediate goals, today's focus) feeding a WSJF-style order. Goal alignment is the value term, existing triggers and promises are time-criticality, and sizing is the denominator. Today's focus is a decaying boost, inside WIP limits that already exist |
| (c) the operator's mood shifts daily | new | nothing records what matters to the operator today. The proposal is a **focus**: the operator's own words for today, dated, carrying a weight that halves every `focus-half-life` (default one day) and reaches nothing after a few days unless restated. It is never a disposition and authorizes nothing |
| (d) intermediate and long-term goals ground the operator and do not shift daily | new | nothing records goals. The proposal is a **goal stack**: each goal in the operator's words, with its horizon (intermediate: weeks; long-term: a quarter or more) and a review date. It changes only on the operator's prompt and is reviewed at a set cadence, riding the weekly grooming, never daily. Every item the home recommends or promotes is shown with the goal it serves ("serves G2"), or marked as serving none. That grounding is how the goals influence the operator |
| (e) when mood and goals disagree | new, and the operator's own rule | the prompt states this operator's rule: goals ground the decision, and mood is a weight that fades. The proposed default for this operator is that goal alignment orders first and today's focus orders within it. A focus that departs from every goal is not silently obeyed or ignored: it is shown once with the goal it departs from, and the operator either adopts it as a goal or keeps it for today. Another operator may weigh the two the other way, so the conflict rule itself is a per-operator setting, not the mechanism's |

The code computes the order from records. **Per operator, never in a craft or the core**: the goal stack, today's focus, and this operator's settings (the conflict rule, the half-life, horizon weights), plus any decision record of the operator's own that the mechanism admits as an overlay, which may add or order outcomes but not change its inputs. Where the per-operator store lives is (h) |

Limits to state in the play-book: the order recommends and never decides (a form, never an authority, `dmc:preference-kind`); every item still becomes work only by its own question or the operator's prompt. Goals are the operator's words and the agent never writes one. An inferred focus is a proposal, never the operator's statement. The order is only as good as the goal stack: with no goals recorded, today's order is unchanged, and the home says so once.

Measured, or not claimed (Guide §12 item 7): the play-book counts, per weekly review, how many items the home recommended or promoted and how many the operator took up, by goal, so the strategy's effect is read and not asserted.
