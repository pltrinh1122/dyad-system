**Ambiguous, with the reading taken:**
- **A1. Where the set lives, and what landing it means.** The desk's charter writes `site/` (infra zone). Every
  tracked path in this repository is visible to anyone who can read it, so landing the canvas on `main` publishes it
  in effect, while §5 says a canvas is accepted, never published. Read: **the set lives at `site/lean-canvas/`**, one
  file per section (`problem.md` … `unfair-advantage.md`) and `index.md` (the labels, the riskiest assumption and the
  fit verdict). It stays on its branch and in its PR until your done-`Y`. That `Y` both accepts it as the foundation
  and lets it land, visibly. If you want it accepted but never visible, say so and the plan keeps it off `main`.
- **A2. Who the product is for.** The record names "an Operator who delegates building and operating work to an AI
  agent and stays accountable for the result", which #754 found `too-broad`. Read, every word a **hypothesis**:
  - **segment:** a technical person who already runs a CLI coding agent (Claude Code or another) on repositories they
    answer for;
  - **early adopters:** those among them who today review every agent change by hand before it lands, because they
    have been burned by one they didn't.
  You may amend the segment. Every other section follows from it.
- **A3. Evidence versus hypothesis.** The repo proves what the system does, and that one operator uses it daily. It
  proves nothing about any other reader. Read: an entry is **evidence** only when it cites a path or count about the
  system itself (the solution, its costs, its metrics as counted). Everything said about the segment, its words, its
  channels or its willingness to pay is a **hypothesis**. The riskiest assumption, named in `index.md`, is that the
  early adopters feel the review burden strongly enough to accept a `Y` on every plan and every Done.
- **A4. Revenue.** No record shows a price or a sale. Read: "none, and why" — free and open, offered to practitioners.
  This passes revenue's `empty` honestly; it is still a hypothesis that this is the intended model.
- **A5. The fence.** No person, client, employer or private repository appears in any piece. The operator appears
  only as "the operator", the role.

**Definition of done (proposed).**
- Ten files under `site/lean-canvas/`: nine sections, each named after its record, and `index.md`. Every entry is
  labelled **H** or **E**, each **E** citing its path or count.
- Each section judged by its record and the canvas by the gate and the fit record. The aim is `ready` throughout, with
  any section that cannot honestly pass reported as it stands, never dressed up to pass.
- One infra-zone PR on its branch, not landed before your done-`Y`, which accepts the canvas as the foundation.
- Nothing posted or sent outside the repository.

## Intent as read, revision 2 (provenance #672, entry 2; extended by entry 3)
The operator's words: "there will be multiple segments of who it's for because ultimately everyone will benefit to some
degree - some more than others. which segments we target will depend on the intent and phase of the go to market. i
want to identify all of the segments that have significant problems - not just early adopters". No `Y` or `N`: entry 2
is a `prompt`, and the intent question stands, revised.

**What changes.** Revision 1 chose one segment. Read now: **the set maps every segment with a significant problem,
and targets none.** Targeting is a later decision, made by the go-to-market's intent and phase.

**A false premise in the records** (#671's, found by this reading). A lean canvas holds one segment: the fit record's
`misaimed` and the problem record's `unranked` both assume one. A set covering many segments fails them as written. Read:
- **segment-specific sections are written once per segment**: customer segments (who, and the early adopters within),
  problem (the top one to three, in their words, with the existing alternatives);
- **product sections are written once, shared**: solution, cost structure, unfair advantage. These are facts about the
  system, mostly evidence;
- **targeting sections wait for a target**: unique value proposition, channels, key metrics and revenue. Each says why
  it is for a segment, and is written per targeted segment in a later row;
- the fit record is applied per segment once that segment is targeted. Until then `index.md` says it is not yet judged.
  Saying this in `collateral.md` is a craft-zone change, so it is its own PR beside this set's.

**Significant, defined.** Read, as a hypothesis for you to amend: a segment's problem is **significant** when all three
hold:
1. the segment answers for work an AI agent did;
2. an unchecked error costs it something real (money, safety, reputation, a regulator, a user's trust);
3. it happens often enough to recur in the segment's week.
Each segment is rated on those three, the rating labelled H.

**Candidate segments**, all H. For each: whether the system serves it **today** (evidence: what the repo does) or not
yet. Mapped, never chosen:
| # | segment | why the problem may be significant | served today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | solo developers running a CLI coding agent on repositories they own | they alone answer for every agent change | yes: the core craft (E) |
| 2 | open-source maintainers receiving agent-written contributions | they answer for every merge to their users | in part: review and provenance, not contributors' agents |
| 3 | engineering leads answering for a small team's agent-written changes | errors reach production under their name | yes, one operator per dyad (E: Rule-2) |
| 4 | platform, DevOps and SRE engineers letting agents act on hosts | a wrong command can take a service down | yes: host actions classed and logged (E: Rule-8, the sysadmin craft) |
| 5 | engineers in regulated industries (finance, health, public sector) | they must show who approved what | in part: every approval is recorded verbatim (E: Rule-7); no certification is claimed |
| 6 | security and audit teams reviewing agent activity | they cannot audit what was never recorded | in part: the record exists; no audit tooling is claimed |
| 7 | consultancies and agencies delivering agent-assisted work to their clients | they answer to a client for every deliverable | yes, as in 1 and 3 |
| 8 | researchers needing reproducible, attributable agent-assisted work | the result must survive review | in part: provenance and decision records (E) |
| 9 | writers, analysts and marketers delegating drafts to agents | a published error is theirs | in part: the chronicle craft (E); today it needs git and a CLI |
| 10 | leaders answering for an organization's adoption of agents | they own the risk without seeing the work | not yet: one operator per dyad; no organization view is claimed |

**Definition of done (revision 2, proposed).**
- `site/lean-canvas/customer-segments.md` and `problem.md`, one entry per segment above as amended. Each entry is
  judged by its record, one segment at a time.
- `solution.md`, `cost-structure.md` and `unfair-advantage.md`, shared, judged by their records.
- `index.md`: the significance criterion, each segment's rating and riskiest assumption, the labels, which sections
  wait for a target and why, and the fit marked "not yet judged, no target".
- One infra-zone PR (the set) and one craft-zone PR (`collateral.md`: a set may hold many segments, section records
  apply per segment, and the fit applies per targeted segment), neither landed before your done-`Y`.
- Nothing posted or sent outside the repository. The privacy fence of revision 1 (A5) holds: every segment is a class,
  never a person or a named organization.
