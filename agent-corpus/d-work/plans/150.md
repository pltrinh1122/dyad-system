## Dialectic
**Thesis: keep *Placet* and re-source it to academic assemblies.** The mapping to three answers,
the one quality that set it apart in #149, stays whole, and the word needs no rework.
**Antithesis:** the Operator asked for *less* religious connotation, and a citation cannot change
what a reader hears. The word is the same, and among clients the Vatican II association will
dominate the Oxford one. A secular name should carry the invariant *by its own meaning*.
**Synthesis: *Countersign*.** It needs no gloss:
- An act stands only when a second, independent party signs after the first. That is
  proposer ≠ disposer in one word, and it names the pair (the dyad) without saying "dyad".
- Its native practice has all three answers: countersign, refuse, or countersign subject to a
  rider (N8).
- It maps onto the three modes:
  - explicit: a countersignature per act;
  - implicit: a standing, countersigned mandate;
  - automatic: a countersigned release of the run-book;
  - escalation: anything unsigned returns for signature.
- It is also literal in both repos. The Agent's evidence block, plan or proposal is the first
  signature, and the Operator's `Y` is the countersignature (Rule-2 Binding; dsys's hash-bound
  AUTHORIZE, E2).

**What it concedes:**
- The password sense of "countersign" is real but rare in a business setting.
- It sounds procedural, which may be a merit for governance and a cost for vision.

## Recommendation
**Countersign Architecture.** Runners-up:
- **Placitum**, if a Latin register is wanted without the ecclesiastical association.
- **Sign-off**, if client legibility outweighs precision (it loses standing authority and triage).

Verify the *(gk)* sources before external use, and run a trademark search.
