# Plan #112 — LICENSE: the most permissive

Base commit of `main`: de5ef95. Refs #111 (the root-file refresh this rides beside; same zone,
separate d-work and PR — a separate prompt, a separate plan-`Y`).

## Intent as read
"Select the most permissive for LICENSE." The root `LICENSE` is MIT today (copyright 2026
pltrinh1122). MIT is permissive but conditional: every copy must carry the copyright and
permission notice. "Most permissive" reads as: no conditions at all — no attribution, no notice
retention, no copyleft, every right granted.

## Candidates, falsified
- **The Unlicense** — a public-domain dedication plus, for jurisdictions that do not recognize
  one, an unconditional fallback grant (use, copy, modify, publish, distribute, sublicense, sell)
  with a warranty disclaimer. OSI-approved (SPDX `Unlicense`). No conditions whatsoever.
- **0BSD** / **MIT-0** — permissive licenses with the attribution/notice clause deleted; no
  conditions either, but they remain licenses (copyright retained), not a dedication.
- **CC0-1.0** — a dedication too, but its explicit patent-rights reservation and the resulting
  OSI non-approval make it the wrong instrument for software.
- Anything with a notice clause (MIT, BSD-2/3, ISC, Apache-2.0) is by definition less permissive.

## Decision (Operator, 2026-09-22): 0BSD
Asked to help decide between 0BSD and The Unlicense, the Agent recommended 0BSD: identical in
permission (no attribution, no notice, no conditions), but a plain license rather than a
public-domain dedication — uniform in every jurisdiction and accepted by every intake policy,
where dedications are refused by some. The Operator chose 0BSD. (The row's title, immutable,
names the first candidate; this plan and the disposition carry the choice.)

## Falsification
Attack: a public-domain dedication is void in jurisdictions with no such concept, leaving those
users with *no* grant — less permissive there than MIT. Result: refuted — The Unlicense's second
paragraph is exactly the fallback: an unconditional license "in perpetuity of all present and
future rights" for anyone the dedication does not reach; 0BSD-equivalent in effect.

Attack: 0BSD is the cleaner instrument — one short OSI-approved license, no dedication theory.
Result: survives as the strongest counter, scoped — 0BSD and The Unlicense grant the same
rights with the same absence of conditions; The Unlicense additionally relinquishes copyright
itself where it can, so on the question actually asked ("most permissive") it is the maximum,
and where it cannot it collapses to the 0BSD position. The Operator may prefer 0BSD for its
conventionality; the difference is form, not permission.

Attack (on the chosen 0BSD): keeping a copyright line makes it "less permissive" than a
dedication. Result: refuted on the question that matters — the line asserts authorship, it
imposes no condition; 0BSD's grant is unconditional with or without it, and the line is what
keeps the file legible to scanners as SPDX `0BSD` with a named author.
