## Survivor

exercise's `survey`, `group`, `inventory`, `plan` and `verify`; and the re-test of every shipped fence (the standing
audit's phase 2, which is hardening's `verify` generalized). Audit writes only `<instance>/audits/`, opens and fixes
nothing, and every finding is a proposed row. What leaves it goes to the owner of the domain it reads:
- **counsel** — the Rule-5 sweeps and the self-diagnostic's prose-claim levels (L3–L4), which read the Rules and the
  countersign model already in its domain; drift item "Rules edited since the last sweep".
- **engineer** — the standing regression (phase 1) and the self-diagnostic's coded-check levels (L1–L2).
- **program** — drift items "rows with no activity" and "unmerged remote branches", which are its board.
- **operate** — drift items "stale presence" (`dyad session reconcile`, #431) and "host documents older than the
  running kernel".
- Each seat that owns a fence domain runs hardening's `execute` for a mode audit planned, on the Operator's
  disposition of that row.

## Rule-10
**Path.** Adopt the survivor: audit is the incident ledger and its hardening, plus the fence re-test. The standing
audit becomes a composite whose phases are run by their domain owners, and the self-diagnostic splits by kind of check.
**Counter.** Attack 6: the third line stops reading the Rules and the model, so the seat that writes them grades them
with no second reader before the Operator. Keeping the wide charter preserves that second reader.
**Reconciliation.** The independence a Rule demands is the Operator's, and it is unchanged. Audit keeps the check that
does not depend on re-reading prose: it sees every outcome through the log. The lost second reader is conceded and
written into the charter's own record, so a later Operator can restore it as a charter edit.
