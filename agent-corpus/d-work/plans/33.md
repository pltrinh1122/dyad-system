## Finding — the claim is already planned, more thoroughly than this session could redo it
`agent-corpus/d-work/plans/27.md` (peer): proposes `crafts/sysarch/rules/failure.md`, a new Tended
rule in the sysarch craft, with three properties — *early is a place, not a moment* (detect a fault
at the highest rung it is knowable on: declaration → transaction → run → use), *loud is named and
attributable* (every fault, and every skip, prints a line naming itself), *fatal is not early* (a
run reports every fault; it does not abort at the first one). Its own falsification checks the
claim against four already-ratified positions that a naive "abort on first fault" reading would
contradict (`crafts/syseng/rules/invariants.md` p3's `assert` ban, Rule-20 p2's skip-not-fail on an
absent store, Rule-11 p7's bundle skip, Rule-7's warn-not-fail on a missing provenance record) and
resolves the tension rather than hand-waving it.

This session's own falsification of *that* plan, not a rubber stamp (Rule-9, easy agreement):
- **"Sysarch is the wrong home; this binds the Agent's process (Rule-4), so it is Rule-21 (guard
  containment) territory."** Refuted: Rule-21 (guard containment) and Rule-17 (projection) were
  both retired into sysarch Tended rules at #160 (`guards.md`, `projection.md`) — an architecture
  rule about *how checks are built and reported* is exactly this precedent's shape, not a process
  Rule like Rules 1–3. The Operator's own word, "architecture", matches where the corpus already
  puts this class of rule.
- **"'Early is a place, not a moment' stretches the phrase past its plain meaning to dodge a
  contradiction."** Refuted: the standard engineering sense of "fail early" is detect-as-soon-as-
  knowable (validate at the boundary, not deep in a call stack), not abort-on-first-error; the two
  are genuinely different claims, and the corpus's own four ratified skip/warn positions are
  evidence for which one it already holds, not a contradiction being explained away.
- **"'Loud' should mean every issue becomes a hard failure, and #27 waters that down."** Refuted:
  standard usage of "fail loud" opposes *silent* failure, not *soft* failure; a `skip <kind>:
  <reason>` line is loud (printed, attributable) without being fatal. Rule-20 property 2 and
  Rule-11 property 7 already draw exactly this line.
- **No honest counter found** to #27's placement or its three properties as stated.
