**Ambiguous, with the reading taken:**
- **A1. The whole canvas, or each box.** You mentioned element records in #668. Read: **one record for the whole
  canvas now.** Its tests run box by box, in the order a canvas is filled in, and they also check that the boxes fit
  together (the solution answers the problems, the channel reaches the segment). Records for single boxes come later,
  each by its own prompt.
- **A2. A canvas is made of hypotheses, and the gate demands evidence** (supersede, small). The gate's `unanchored`
  fails any claim with no path, count or test behind it. Most of a young canvas is guesses, so the gate would fail
  nearly every canvas. Read: **an entry labelled as a hypothesis claims nothing as fact, so `unanchored` passes it**;
  an entry stated as fact still needs its anchor. That changes one sentence of #668's gate (its `unanchored`
  condition) and one line of `collateral.md`. The canvas record then requires that the labels are there.
- **A3. The value-proposition box.** It is a pitch in one sentence. Read: the canvas record judges it with the pitch
  record's `no-reader`, `cold` and `abstract`. The rest of the canvas gets no emotional tests.
- **A4. Boxes a product may honestly leave empty.** dyad-system, for example, may have no revenue. Read: "none, and
  why" passes a box. An empty or vague box fails.

**The proposed outcomes**, in precedence (after the gate passes; the first that fails gives the outcome):
1. `unmarked`: the entries do not say which are hypotheses and which are evidence, or the riskiest assumption is not
   named. Label them.
2. `no-segment`: the customer segment is too broad to find one person in it, or the early adopters are not named.
3. `no-problem`: the top one to three problems are not stated in the segment's own terms, with what they use today
   instead (the existing alternatives).
4. `no-uvp`: the unique value proposition is not one sentence a member of the segment would recognize, tied to the
   top problem. Judged with the pitch record's `no-reader`, `cold` and `abstract`.
5. `unmapped`: a solution feature answers no listed problem, or a listed problem has no feature: a solution-first
   canvas.
6. `unreachable`: no channel that actually reaches the early adopters.
7. `no-model`: the revenue or cost box is empty or vague. "None, and why" passes.
8. `unmeasured`: the key metrics count activity, not the segment getting the value (vanity metrics).
9. `copyable`: the unfair advantage is something a competitor could copy or buy (a feature, being first). "None yet"
   stated honestly passes.
10. `ready`: every test holds. The canvas goes to the operator's publication question and is never published by the
    record.

**Definition of done (proposed).**
- `crafts/chronicle/dmc/collateral-lean-canvas.jsonld` with the outcomes above as amended, its home `collateral.md`
  (§2 extended);
- the gate's `unanchored` and `collateral.md` amended for hypotheses (A2), and the falsification record
  `falsification/rules/collateral.md` extended;
- one craft-zone PR, the dmc and craft guards passing;
- evidence: a sketch lean canvas for dyad-system, drafted only from its identity record and README, judged by the gate
  and the new record, in the PR body only. It is not published, and it is not a canvas you have approved.

## Intent as read, revision 3 (provenance #671, entry 3)
The operator's words: "the lean canvas is sufficient for helping ground any product marketing pitch. if there are
gaps, then we'll augment them accordingly, e.g. product vision board. the intent is that we have all of the
"foundational" elements to support the crafting of any marketing pitch". No `Y` or `N`: entry 3 is a `prompt`, and the
intent question stands, revised.

**What changes.** Revision 2 judged the canvas as one more piece of collateral, a thing put to the reader. Read now:
**the canvas is the foundation a pitch is crafted from.** It is internal and never put to a reader. A pitch's claims
trace back to its sections. So, with revision 2's ten records kept:
- **The fit record's `ready`** now means "ready to ground pitches". Its consequence: the canvas goes to the operator to
  be accepted as the foundation the pitches cite. It is never published.
- **The pitch record gains one outcome, `ungrounded`**, placed after `no-reader`: the pitch's reader, problem or value
  proposition does not trace to a section of an accepted canvas. This is a small supersede of #668's
  `collateral-pitch`.
- **`collateral.md` gains a grounding table** showing which canvas section grounds which pitch test, and it names the
  gaps the claim's falsification finds (below) as gaps for later augmentation. It builds none of them now.

**Grounding map** (pitch test ← canvas section):
| pitch test | grounded by | covered? |
|---|---|---|
| `no-reader` | customer segments, early adopters | yes |
| `cold` (the felt problem, in their words) | problem | yes, if the problem is in the segment's words (`not-theirs`) |
| `manufactured` (no fear beyond the real stakes) | problem, existing alternatives | in part: the stakes, what failing to solve it costs the reader, are in no box |
| `abstract` (a concrete moment from the reader's day) | none | **gap** |
| `no-pull` (what changes, and a next step) | unique value proposition, solution; channels for the next step | yes |
| `uncountered` (the strongest objection) | existing alternatives, unfair advantage | in part: an alternative implies an objection, but the objection itself is in no box |
| the gate's `invented`, `unanchored` | every section, labelled hypothesis or evidence | yes |

### Falsification of the operator's claim (Rule-9: "the lean canvas is sufficient for helping ground any product marketing pitch")
| id | attack | result | survivor |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | a pitch needs a concrete moment from the reader's day (`abstract`), and no canvas box holds a scene, a story or a before and after | confirmed | gap 1, **the reader's moment**: named in `collateral.md` for augmentation (a customer story or scenario; a product vision board does not hold one either) |
| G2 | a pitch must meet the strongest objection (`uncountered`); the canvas lists alternatives, not objections | survives, scoped | the existing alternatives ground the commonest objection ("I already use X"); gap 2, **objections**, is named for the rest |
| G3 | emotion needs stakes; the problem box states the problem, not what leaving it unsolved costs | survives, scoped | gap 3, **the stakes**, is named; the problem record's `not-theirs` already asks for the segment's own words, where stakes usually show |
| G4 | the canvas is a startup tool, so "any" pitch overreaches: a pitch for a free, open product has no revenue or price | refuted | revenue's "none, and why" passes (L6); nothing in the grounding map reads revenue or cost |
| G5 | grounding makes every pitch wait for an accepted canvas | survives, scoped | that is the operator's intent ("foundational"); a pitch drafted before then fails `ungrounded` and says which section it lacks |

**Verdict:** the claim **survives, scoped**. The canvas grounds a pitch's reader, problem, value, change and next step.
It leaves three named gaps, the reader's moment, objections and stakes, for augmentation by later records.
