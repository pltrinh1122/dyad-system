# Plan #410 — a 'personas' classification: evaluation and falsification

## Intent as read
Evaluate whether a `personas` classification fits this system, for grouping concurrent
initiatives or programs. The Operator's claim to falsify: it is unnecessary, because more sessions
than the six seats would already exceed the span of control.

## Falsification of the claim

| # | attack | result |
|---|--------|--------|
| 1 | Span of control bounds a node's *direct* lines, not the system's session count. M4 is a tree (#368): the leader reads at most seven lines (MANDATES.md, program row), and each member-cos keeps its own cabinets under it. Live today: distribute held two cabinets beside the six seats, eight sessions, no span breached. | **refuted** — sessions may exceed six without exceeding any span; the ceiling is per node |
| 2 | Sessions are not initiatives. One seat carries many rows: distribute's presence held 9 rows under #345; the ledger has 69 rows with `refs: parent #N`. So "more initiatives than seats" is the normal state and says nothing about span. | **refuted** — the claim conflates the two |
| 3 | Grouping of concurrent initiatives already exists, three ways: the parent row (`refs: parent #N`, 69 rows), the mandate (a plan-Y's accreted set, named in the Done question), and the charter's domain (one function per seat; the seat name's optional `<topic>` for one subject under it, cos `rules/naming.md`). A fourth axis would need a reason none of these covers. None found in the ledger. | **confirmed** — personas add no grouping the system lacks |
| 4 | The term misfits. In the industry sense a persona is a modelled *user* archetype (UX), or a behavioral style an agent adopts; an org decomposes by *function* and *program*, which M4 already does. A persona axis across seats would be a matrix organization, the shape #368 §2 weighed and set aside for a tree. | **confirmed** — the fit is poor as a term, and the shape it implies was already declined |
| 5 | Strongest case for personas: the 13 retired `cab-*` seats carry no function, so history cannot be grouped by charter. | **survives, scoped** — that is a naming gap of history, served by the `<topic>` slot going forward, not a classification |

## Survivor
No `personas` classification. The Operator's conclusion (unnecessary) stands; the reason given does
not: span of control is per node of the tree, so sessions beyond six are admissible under a member-cos,
and concurrent initiatives are grouped by parent row, mandate and charter domain (plus `<topic>`).
Span is breached only when the *leader's* board exceeds seven lines or a member's exceeds its own
ceiling, which the program charter already names as an escalation.

## Survivor (revision 2)
At any moment exactly one session holds a seat, so the six standing seats hold at most six sessions.
What can exceed six is the number of *seats*: cabinets under a member-cos, each one session, each a seat
in the form with a topic, bounded by that member's span. Over time many sessions hold one seat. The
outcome of revision 1 is restated: not "more sessions for the six seats" but "more seats than six, one
session each, under the members".
