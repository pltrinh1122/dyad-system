# Plan #330 — design, architecture, framework: the three layers, and the d-work ↔ countersign mapping

*Rule-15 phase 1. Base commit of `main`: 9c15e98 (2026-10-17). refs: 309 296 290 156 152. Seat `ds-cabinet-cloud-1`.*

## Intent as read
Two things, in order. **First, alignment**: the Operator asks that we agree on what *design*, *architecture*
and *framework* mean before anything is written, because the consolidation and refactor of the Dyad System
will be organized by those three words. The plan therefore proposes definitions, grounded in how this
corpus already uses the words, and the plan-`Y` is the alignment; an `N` with text amends the
definitions before any document is written. **Second, the mapping**: explain how the d-work framework
relates to the countersign architecture (the Countersign core schema and interaction model) and the
countersign craft (the Tended craft `crafts/countersign/` that packages them), and document that mapping
as a record the consolidation effort can read. Not read as: changing any Rule, craft, guard or projector —
the document elevates what the refactor must decide; it decides nothing (detect, don't dispose).

## The three terms, as proposed (the alignment)
Each is defined by what it answers, where this corpus already keeps it, and how it is checked.
| term | answers | where it lives here today | checked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **design** | *why* — the choices made and the alternatives refused, with their reasons | falsification records (Rule-9), plan files (Rule-15), the ADR audits (#252, #302), the "design" paragraphs of `crafts/sysarch/rules/` (the README calls them "the design behind these kernels") | inference only: falsified, swept, never guarded |
| **architecture** | *what* — the entities, their stores and fields, the relations between them, the states they carry, and the boundaries that contain them | the countersign core schema (eight entities, one attribute); the sysarch craft's rules (stores, guards, references, projection); `dyadlib.STATES/TRANSITIONS/INPUTS`; each guard's `FIELDS`; the reference register | mechanically: guards check the shape, projectors draw it, invariants hold it at import |
| **framework** | *how* — the procedure a party follows to produce and move instances of the architecture: the Rules that bind the process, the play-books and run-books, the CLI verbs, the forms of question and answer | the core craft's Agent Rules (Rule-3, 7, 15, 16, 2, 10), the six play-books, `dyad dwork` and `dyad session`, the counter-prompt forms and the answer grammar | by the transaction guards at the moment of each act (the plan gate, the main fence, the provenance check), and by inference for conduct |

The three stack without overlapping: design justifies the architecture; the architecture is what the
framework operates on; the framework produces the instances the architecture describes, and the design
records why it could not have been otherwise. A **craft** is orthogonal to the three: a packaging and
versioning unit that may carry all of them (a craft's `rules/` hold design and framework, its schema or
`FIELDS` hold architecture, its guards hold the checks). A **system** (dyad-system, countersign-system,
dsys) is one instance of all three under one Operator. Tests of the split: a statement that names an
alternative is design; one a projector could draw is architecture; one with a verb a party performs is
framework. A sentence that is two of these at once is the refactor's finding, not a fault of the
definitions.

## The mapping, as read now (what the document will state in full)
- **Countersign** is an *architecture* (`rules/schema.md`: party, act, proposal, countersignature,
  mandate, release, event, escalation; the mode attribute) **plus** a *framework* (`rules/interaction.md`:
  five primitives, eight imperatives, one lifecycle per mode, the answer grammar, preferences classed as
  form or authority), packaged as a *craft* (Tended, not bundled, 0.3.0), with its design in
  `falsification/schema.md` and `interaction.md` (F1–F7, R1–R12). It is the **portable** form: every
  Countersign system maps to it *by projection*, keeping its own entities as a profile (F5, F7).
- **d-work** is dyad-system's *framework* (Rule-3 and its neighbours, the play-books, the verbs) over
  dyad-system's own *architecture* (the five records and the machine, as #309's play-book states them),
  with its design in the Rules' records. It is the **concrete** form: a profile of the countersign core
  reached by the projector `crafts/countersign/projectors/project_countersign.py` under the derivation
  rules D1–D8.
- The relation is therefore **realization by projection**, and the mapping has three parts the document
  will table: (1) architecture → architecture: each d-work record and field to the countersign entity and
  field it projects onto, with the derivation rule and its status (exact, derived, stale, missing);
  (2) framework → framework: each machine input (`dyadlib.INPUTS`) and each counter-prompt form to the
  interaction primitive and answer it is, with the imperative that binds it; (3) design → design: where
  the two records of *why* agree and where they diverge (the dyad's two-party model against the core's
  three party kinds; `before` check timing; the preference classification).
- **Known divergences the refactor must decide**, to be elevated, not resolved: D1 derives `explicit`
  for every act although rows carry `mode` since #290; §7's four owed refactors (amend — done by #290;
  a mandate record — not done, `ledger-pr-merge` still prose; a `mode` field — done by #290, projector
  stale; an initiation per row — partly, via `prompt` entries and `--relayed-via`); the core has no
  Initiation entity while d-work's provenance record is one; no d-work entity exists for the
  countersign's *release* or *executor* (dyad-system has no automatic mode); F7 forbids storage migration,
  so "consolidate" can mean *rename the profile* or *adopt the core natively*, and the document states the
  cost of each without choosing.
