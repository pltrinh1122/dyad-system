## Intent as read
Before anyone plans 2.0.0's content, fix what earns a major at all, so the v2.0.0 scope (#610) classifies candidates
against a criterion the operator has ratified rather than one inferred per item.

## What exists today
- `release.md` Classes: **breaking** means "an installing system must change something on upgrade: a verb or a file
  format removed or renamed, a Rule that now refuses what it allowed, an install contract changed". It takes a major
  and never ships without its upgrade step (`port.md`). Every other class takes a minor or a patch.
- `surface.md`: "`frozen`: changing it after 1.0.0 needs 2.0.0", over 228 frozen items. Crafts are out of scope; each
  Tended craft keeps its own number and surface.
- `release.md` Numbers: the bundle takes the highest class among its components and moves the same part.

## The gaps, and the recommended answer to each
| id | question | today | recommended answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Is adding beside a frozen item (a new flag on a frozen verb, a new optional key) major? | `surface.md` says any change; `release.md` makes a new flag a minor | **No.** Major only when an installing system must change something on upgrade. `frozen` reads: "removing, renaming or narrowing it needs a major; adding beside it is a minor." |
| Q2 | Is a feature with no break ever major? | silent | **No.** A major means a breaking change and nothing else. Features alone ship as 1.x minors, however large. |
| Q3 | Is a new fence a break? | "a Rule that now refuses what it allowed" is breaking | **Only when the Rule's text allowed it.** A fence that refuses what a Rule already forbade enforces existing text: a fix, not a break. A Rule change that forbids what its text allowed is a break. |
| Q4 | Must a removal or rename be deprecated first? | silent after 1.0 (#554 and #604 did it for 1.0) | **Yes.** The old form warns, naming the new one, in at least one 1.x minor. The major removes it with its `port.md` upgrade step. |
| Q5 | Does a major cut when its first break is ratified, or are breaks held for a planned major? | a breaking change set releases at once | **Held.** A deprecation lands in any minor; the removal it announces merges only inside a planned major's window, so majors are planned events. This is #610's branch strategy (a): 1.x ends at the major, so no maintenance branch is needed. |
| Q6 | Does a Tended craft's break make the bundle major? | "the bundle moves the same part" | **Only from a component at 1.0 or later.** A Tended craft below 1.0 is unstable by semver. Its break moves the bundle by a minor, and the release question marks it **breaking**. The bundle's major follows the core's major and the majors of components at 1.0 or later. |
