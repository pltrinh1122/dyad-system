## Plan
**The play-book.** `crafts/chronicle/playbooks/alignment-review.md`. Crafts keep play-books beside their rules, as
`crafts/channels/playbooks/` and `crafts/cabinet/playbooks/` do. Its sections:
- **Purpose:** the operator and the agent align on target segments and each target's problems: classes, roots, anchor branch.
- **Inputs:** a canvas set judged by §5, its projection and source trees.
- **Steps:** the four phases of the reading, each with its view, its one question in plain form and how its answer is
  entered (a `prompt` entry; nothing ratifies before the done-`Y`).
- **Knobs:** the named parameters a run may set, each with its default, so that tuning means changing a default:
  - **order**: top-down, or bottom-up;
  - **targets**: at most 3;
  - **batch**: whether phases 1 and 2 share a reply; default no;
  - **depth**: the targets only, or every segment;
  - **tree-first**: a target with no source tree gets one, derived independently, before phase 3; default always.
- **Run record:** what every run leaves in the canvas set, `<canvas set>/reviews/<row>.md`:
  - the knobs as set;
  - each phase's question and your answer, by provenance entry;
  - what was amended;
  - the time per phase, from the row's trace;
  - the agent's proposed tunings, each **H**.
- **Tuning:** a tuning is a craft PR on your prompt that changes a step or a knob's default, citing the run records
  behind it. A play-book changes only that way (Rule-13: recurrence proposes, the operator disposes).
- **Evidence:** the run record and the canvas set's Targets section.

`collateral.md` §5 gains one line pointing to it. The craft guard and the tests see a new Markdown file only.

**This run** is the play-book's first, with knobs at their defaults:
1. **Phase 1:** a review page of the four problem classes, each with its roots and the segments that suffer it. Your
   answer is entered as a `prompt`.
2. **Phase 2:** a review page of segment cards in rank order, each with its phase-1 classes. You pick 1–3 targets and
   settle the tie or the axes where they touch the targets.
3. **Phase 3:** for each target with no source tree, one is derived first by a fresh drafter briefed without any other
   tree, as #685 did, and projected. Then one review page per target, slicing its source tree to its roots, the
   branches that reach them, the anchor branch and its symptoms in the segment's words. You accept, or amend a node,
   and an amended node is written as a superseding node.
4. **Phase 4:** the PRs, then the Done question.

**Mandate scope:** none. The drafters are subagents, not rows, and every tree they derive lands in this row's infra PR.

**The reader (an input to the record):** someone who uses an AI assistant out of the box. They chat with it, ask it to
write, change or do things, and sometimes let it act: edit a file, send a message, run a step. They have no git, no
pipeline, no change control, and no word for "provenance", "version" or "risk class".

**The record, as drafted.**
- Its question: "Would a cursory user of an out-of-the-box AI agent recognize this problem class as their own?"
- Its outcomes, in precedence:
  1. **jargon**: it uses a word the reader does not use (record, provenance, version, intent, risk class, evidence,
     pipeline). Say it in everyday words.
  2. **tool-bound**: it assumes a setup the reader lacks (a repository, CI, a change board). Say it for a chat with
     an assistant, or mark it segment-specific.
  3. **mechanism-framed**: it names how a fix would work rather than what the reader goes through. Say what goes wrong
     for them.
  4. **no-moment**: plain, but the reader could not point to when it happened to them. Give the moment.
  5. **symptom-slid**: made plain, it now names what the reader sees rather than why it happens
     (`collateral-problem-depth` still applies). Restore the cause in plain words.
  6. **plain**: every test holds.
- Each class keeps two layers: the plain statement, which this record judges and which the surfaces and pitches show,
  and the precise roots, by node id, which the trees keep.

**Draft plain statements (each H, judged by the record as drafted).** Phase 1 shows these for your answer:

| K1 | Nothing keeps what the AI did together with what you okayed, so later you can't show either. | You let it tidy a shared document. A week later someone asks what changed and whether you agreed, and the chat is buried. | plain |
| K2 | Nothing checks that what the AI did is what you actually wanted, before you rely on it. | It said "done". You sent the result on, and only later saw it had solved a different problem. | plain |
| K3 | Your "yes" isn't tied to what you said yes to: the AI can do more than you agreed, or change it after you agreed. | You said "go ahead" to a small fix. It also renamed things you didn't ask about. | plain |

**The reference tree, as drafted (every node H):**
```
X0 [symptom] The AI states something false as if it were true.
  X1 [cause] It produces what is likely, not what is checked: a plausible answer and a true one look the same to it.
    X1.1 [cause] Nothing between the AI and you compares the claim with a source.
      X1.1.1 [root-cause] Nothing checks what the AI produced against what is true, or what you wanted, before you rely on it.   → K2, widened
  X2 [cause] It sounds just as sure when it is wrong.
    X2.1 [cause] It does not show which parts it knows, which it inferred and which it filled in.
      X2.1.1 [root-cause] Nothing shows where an answer came from or how sure it is.   → new class K5
  X3 [cause] It answers past what it knows (missing, out of date, or not given to it) instead of saying so.
    → X2.1.1
  X4 [cause] It answered a different question from the one you meant.
    → X1.1.1
  X5 [cause] You could not tell, because checking would cost more than asking did. (attention)
    → X1.1.1
  X6 [cause] Why does it matter at all? You pass the answer on, and you answer for it. (the anchor branch)
    → X1.1.1
    → X2.1.1
    X6.1 [anchor] I answer for the result.   = N7.1.1
```
- **Mapped:** X1.1.1 widens K2 from "what you wanted" to "what is true, or what you wanted". X2.1.1 is not held by
  any class: **K5**, "nothing shows where an answer came from or how sure it is".
- **Not reached:** K1, K3 and K4. Hallucination is a failure of an agent that *answers*; K1, K3 and K4 are failures of
  an agent that *acts*. So the cursory reader knows K2 and K5 from their own use, and K1, K3 and K4 need a bridge:
  "when the AI does things, not just says things".
