**Ambiguous, with the reading taken:**
- **A1. Where the record lives.** Either the chronicle craft (`crafts/chronicle/dmc/`, craft zone: every system that
  installs chronicle gets it) or this instance (`agent-corpus/dmc/`, this system only). Read: **the chronicle craft**.
  The tests are not about dyad-system; "position from purpose" generalises to "the system's own statement of what it
  is for". Its home must be a file that states the outcomes: a new form section, `crafts/chronicle/rules/pitch.md`
  (§7's shape), short, holding the outcomes and nothing the core already says.
- **A2. Sequence against the brief.** The brief says a form section comes "once real copy has shown what holds".
  Your prompt asks for the criterion first. Read: **supersede** that ordering for the criterion only. The section
  states the test and nothing else (no style guide, no structure); those still wait for real copy.
- **A3. What a "pitch" is.** Read: any piece whose job is to answer, for one named reader, "why should I care about
  this?": a README opening, a site hero, a press-release lede, a one-paragraph post. Length does not matter.
- **A4. The shape of the decision.** A dmc answers one question with outcomes. Read: **"What does this pitch need
  before it is put to the operator?"**, judged by inference. The tests run in a fixed order and the first that fails
  gives the outcome. The proposed outcomes, in precedence:
  1. `fenced` — it names a private repository, a client, an employer, a person or personal context: strike it before
     anything else.
  2. `no-reader` — no named reader, or no one question it answers for them: not drafted further.
  3. `invented` — it claims a capability the system's purpose statement and practice do not hold: cut the claim.
  4. `unanchored` — a claim cites no path, command output or count, or was not falsified (Rule-9), or compares with a
     named product it cannot show: anchor it or cut it.
  5. `decorated` — superlatives, an undefined term of art, more than one idea a sentence, or content the reader
     cannot use: rewrite.
  6. `uncountered` — the pitch's strongest objection from a skeptical reader is not stated with how the copy meets it
     (Rule-10's form): add it.
  7. `ready` — every test holds: put it to the operator's publication question (chronicle §6). Ready never publishes.

**Definition of done (proposed).** One record, `crafts/chronicle/dmc/pitch-discernment.jsonld`, with the outcomes
above as amended, its home `crafts/chronicle/rules/pitch.md`, passing the dmc and craft guards, in one craft-zone PR;
the brief in `plans/666.md` is not edited (it is a ratified plan). Evidence: the record applied to the README's opening
lines as a worked judgement, in the PR body and nowhere published, so you can see it discern.
