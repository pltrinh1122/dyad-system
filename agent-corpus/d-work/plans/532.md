# Plan #532 — seats merge on a recorded Done-Y: close the seam between the ledger's authority and the session's permission (falsified)

Base commit of main: (main at opening)

## Intent as read
The Operator, answering finding S9 of plan #531 revision 4: "the seat should not be refusing merges. evaluate the
current failure in the seam when delegating and work. each seat has the authority to merge if it has been granted the
`Y-done` either directly or relayed by `cos`."

## The failure, as observed
- **2026-10-27, #494.** distribute-a603e150 verified the Done-Y on `main` (`Y done (merges PR #530 at fb51aa1…)`). Its
  pinned merge was then refused by **its session's harness permission classifier, "Merge Without Review"**. The seat
  correctly did not route around the block. The cos merged instead, and has merged every seat PR since (#517, #520, #524,
  #525, #526, #527, #530).
- **Earlier, #451.** engineer's tag push was refused by the same classifier as **"Create Public Surface"**. Releases were
  then run by an Operator script, until the `released` workflow (#461) moved publishing off the seats.

## The seam
- **Authority lives in the corpus.** The Operator's Done-Y is given in the cos's chat, recorded verbatim on `main` (the
  row's `disposed` entry naming each PR at its head, plus the provenance record). Rule-2 makes executing it clerical, for
  whichever Agent session executes it.
- **Permission lives in the session.** Each harness judges an outward act against its own transcript and its permission
  rules. In the seat's session there is no Operator turn approving the merge: the cos's relay arrives as a cross-session
  message, which the harness treats as a peer's data, never as the user's word. So the classifier sees a merge nobody in
  that session authorized, and it refuses.
- **Nothing connects the two.** The cos's merges pass only because the Operator's `Y` is typed in the cos's own chat. So
  the cos executes every seat's merge. That contradicts the cabinet's text ("each seat executes its own merges", Guide §2)
  and its availability rule (#432), and it blocks the cos-as-a-mode (#531 G).
- **Rule-1 Boundaries (#23) is right to forbid the shortcut.** A Rule never reads permission out of itself. The bridge has
  to be an Operator-side permission that points at the corpus.

## Options
- **O1. Status quo:** the cos merges every PR. It works, but it breaks the cabinet's text, loads the cos, and blocks G.
- **O2. A broad allow rule** for the merge tool in `.claude/settings.json`. It works, but any seat could then merge with no
  Done-Y at all, because a hosted merge runs no pre-push hook.
- **O3. Attend the seat** for its Done question. It works only where the Operator sits (as with communicate), and spends
  the Operator's span.
- **O4. A guarded merge verb plus a narrow allow rule.** `dyad merge <pr> --head <sha>` reads `origin/main`. It executes
  only when the operative `Y done` on a row names PR `<pr>` at exactly `<sha>`, the PR's live head is that sha, and the
  head is not yet on `main`; otherwise it refuses, printing why. The repository's `.claude/settings.json` allows **that
  verb only** (`Bash(dyad/bin/dyad merge:*)`, and the `./` form), never the raw merge tool. The permission the Operator
  grants then reads: "a seat may merge exactly what the ledger records your Done-Y on".

## Revision 2 (2026-10-30, on the Operator's prompt: "a clear command-and-control protocol that allows my authority to be applied down a strict chain of authority. channel for communicating authority would be separate of the other messaging channels.")
Revision 1's O4 is one act (merge) authorized by one lookup. The Operator asks for the general form: a protocol carrying
the Operator's authority down the tree, on a channel of its own.

### What it collides with: ADR #252 (done)
Revision 4 of #252 decided three planes:
- *announcement*: the bus and presence;
- *command*: "transport none, ever; the edge is the session; the carrier is its chat";
- *interrupt*: the harness's mid-turn message.

Its consequence 1 reads: "Agent-to-agent control is excluded outright … Control flows Operator → node only. What a cos
sends a cabinet is an announcement or a relayed prompt — never an order."

This proposal **reverses #252's transport conclusion** for the command plane. That is the Operator's to decide, and it is
stated here so the reversal is deliberate. It **keeps #252's premise**: what #252 excluded was an order *originated* by
an agent, a node acting on an instruction no Operator edge authorized. A chain in which every order carries an Operator's
recorded disposition as its root, and every hop can only narrow it, originates nothing. Its M1 objection (an unread
command is indistinguishable from a refused one, so the command plane needs a delivery guarantee) is answered by the
protocol's obligation to read and acknowledge (P4 below).

### The protocol: C2, a chain of authority on its own channel
- **P1. The channel.** An order store, `<instance>/d-work/orders/<id>.md`: one file per order, append-only, written only by
  ledger-only commits under the main fence (Rule-3). It carries **authority and nothing else**. The other channels carry
  none: the bus refuses anything disposition-shaped (#303's guard), `send_message` is at most a doorbell ("order <id>
  waiting"), and presence and status are announcements. An instruction that is not an order on this channel authorizes
  nothing, however it is worded.
- **P2. The root.** Every chain starts at an Operator disposition already recorded verbatim: a `disposed` entry on a row
  and its provenance entry (Rule-7). The root order is issued by the seat in whose chat that disposition was given (the
  cos, or an attended seat), and it cites `prov:#<row>/<n>`. No root, no order.
- **P3. The chain.** Each order names `issuer`, `recipient`, `act`, `target` and `parent` (the order it derives from, or the
  root). It is strict:
  - the issuer of a hop is the recipient of its parent;
  - a hop may only narrow its parent: the same act or a subset of it, the same target or part of it, never wider;
  - the recipient's charter fence (`MANDATES.md`) must cover the act.
  The tree is Operator → cos → secretary → desk, and an order never skips upward or sideways.
- **P4. Delivery and acknowledgement.** A seat reads the orders addressed to it at session start and at every prompt
  (`dyad session touch` reports them). It answers each with exactly one acknowledgement appended to the order: `executed
  <evidence>` or `refused <reason>`. An order left unacknowledged past the stale window shows on the issuer's board, so
  silence is visible, never consent.
- **P5. Execution.** `dyad order execute <id>` verifies the whole chain mechanically: the root entry exists, every hop
  links and narrows, the recipient is this seat, the charter covers the act, and the target is still as the root named it
  (for a merge, the PR's live head equals the head in the `Y done`). Only then does it perform the act, and it writes the
  acknowledgement. The repository's `.claude/settings.json` allows that verb only (`Bash(dyad/bin/dyad order execute:*)`):
  the one Operator-side permission bridging the ledger's authority to the session's (Rule-1 #23 preserved: the permission
  is the Operator's PR, never read out of a Rule).
- **P6. Acts an order may carry.** Initially `merge` (on a `Y done`) and `push-branch`. Never carried, whatever the root:
  a destructive host action (Rule-8 keeps its own question), a release (Rule-11), a preference value, or an intake's
  admission. These stay direct questions to the Operator. A new act is added by a plan, never by a seat.

### Added to the protocol
- **P7. The quick-check.** `dyad authority check <act> <target> [--files <paths>]`, read-only, for a seat that cannot
  honestly decide. It answers with one of three verdicts and the evidence behind it:
  - `covered`, naming the covering chain: the root's verbatim words (`prov:#<row>/<n>`), the plan's mutation and files
    list, the mandate scope's rule and ceiling (with the count used), and the charter fence row;
  - `not covered: <the first test that failed>`, which means ask: the escalate edge (Rule-3);
  - `never`, for an act on the never-list (P6), which is always a direct question to the Operator.
  It reads only `main`, writes nothing, needs no permission, and is the same check `order execute` runs before acting. A
  seat in doubt runs it *before* acting and cites its output in its evidence.
- **P8. Reconciliation (audit's).** `dyad authority audit [--since <sha>]`, run by the audit seat and on demand. It maps
  every outward act on `main` back to its authority and reports what maps to none. Checked:
  - every merge commit maps to an executed order or a recorded `Y done` naming that head;
  - every PR's files fall inside its plan's `files touched`;
  - every accreted row is inside its mandate's rule and ceiling;
  - every `executed` acknowledgement has its act on `main`.
  The findings become incident rows by audit's existing play-book. It detects and never blocks: a guard warning, never a
  failure, true to the good-faith posture.
- **P9. Supersession.** An order's root is the *operative* disposition. A later disposition on the same row that refuses,
  re-asks or replaces it (an `N`, a new `Y done` on a moved head, a revoked charter) voids every order derived from it.
  `order execute` and P7 refuse a void root, and `order list` shows it void.
