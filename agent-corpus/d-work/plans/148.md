## 1. Criteria
Adapted from dsys's own lexicon tests (`glossary.md:31-35`, retired terms: L1 denotation, L2
misreading, L3 collision), plus two the intent adds:
- **N1 denotes.** The name names what the architecture *is*: human–agent work across explicit,
  implicit and automatic modes, held together by human disposition.
- **N2 not misread.** It is not easily read as something narrower or different.
- **N3 no collision.** No clash with the two repos' own terms (#142 §2), existing products, or
  established technical terms.
- **N4 client-legible.** It works in the #143 offer to organizations.
- **N5 durable.** It survives the architecture growing: many humans, fleets, and a shift of work
  toward automatic mode.

## 3. Dialectic

**Thesis: keep "Dyad System Architecture".** It is already the dsys document's title and the
dyad-system repo's name. It honours the lineage (the Dyad Practice, dyad-bond) and the claim both
repos make: the dyad (human plus agent plus R) is the irreducible unit, and "no capability curve
crosses the containment" (`glossary.md:10-17`). Renaming has a real cost in docs, tags and habit,
and a new name risks losing the relational point that makes this more than an agent framework.

**Antithesis: rename, because the incumbent fails at the one job a name has.** It already caused a
refuted claim (#142). Its two referents, an architecture and a repo, both call themselves "the Dyad
System". dsys itself retired the *command* `dyad` because "`dyad <verb>` reads as addressing the
Dyad", and a name that misleads at the prompt misleads in a proposal. It names the unit, not what
makes the unified design distinct. For the #143 client, a multi-person organization, "dyad"
(exactly two members) reads as a mismatch until explained. The strongest single candidate is
**Disposition Architecture**: it names the invariant that spans all three modes and every edge, it
means the same thing in both repos, and no existing technical term claims it.

**Synthesis: name the architecture by its invariant, keep "dyad" for the unit it governs.**
- The **architecture** becomes **Disposition Architecture**: what holds explicit, implicit and
  automatic work together is that every consequential act traces to a human disposition, given per
  turn, in advance (standing), or beforehand (codified), with exceptions escalated back.
- The **dyad** stays the architecture's *unit*: "a Disposition Architecture for dyads and fleets of
  dyads". The lineage and the superset claim are kept, placed where they are true.
- The **repos keep their names.** `dyad-system` is an instance and `dsys` a CLI. Neither is "the
  architecture", which removes the name trap without renaming anything that exists.
- **The concession (N2):** "disposition" also means temperament. The name needs a one-line
  definition wherever it first appears to a client ("a disposition is a human decision the system
  records and enforces"). And the client-facing *product* may carry a plainer name, like #3,
  without the architecture losing its precise one. That split is common: an architecture named for
  its principle, a product named for its buyer.

**Falsification of the synthesis**
| # | attack | result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Automatic mode has no disposition per signal, so "Disposition Architecture" misdenotes it. | **Refuted.** The run-book or flow is itself disposed (authored, promoted under an AUTHORIZE disposition, dsys I-8), and its exceptions return to triage dispositions. A disposition is *upstream* of every automatic act rather than absent |
| 2 | "Disposition" collides with one repo's meaning. | **Refuted.** #142 §2 classes it "overlap": the same core in both (an Operator's answer), differing only in typing. It is the one shared term without a collision |
| 3 | Dropping "dyad" loses the superset claim, that the dyad is never reducible to the agent. | **Survives, conceded and placed.** The claim is kept by naming the dyad as the unit, and "Disposition" encodes its operative half: only the human disposes |
| 4 | A new name costs more than it fixes. | **Refuted, scoped.** Nothing existing is renamed (both repos and all tags keep their names); the new name applies only to the unified architecture that does not yet exist. The cost is one definition line |

## 4. Recommendation
**Disposition Architecture**, with the dyad as its governed unit and a plainer product name
reserved for clients. The incumbent stays as the name of dsys's *current document*, since it is a
historical record and records are not renamed. The strongest runner-up is **Governed Agency
Architecture**, if client legibility is weighted above precision.
