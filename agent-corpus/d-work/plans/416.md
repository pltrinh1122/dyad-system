# Plan #416 — the organization craft's new name: `org` vs `fleet` vs `cabinet`

## Intent as read
#415 settled that the craft at `crafts/cos/` is renamed (the `cos` collision with the client systems'
chief-of-staff craft, and `cos` doubling as a role term). The Operator first said `org`, then asked for a
dialectical evaluation of `org`, `fleet` and `cabinet` before the rename row opens.

## What the craft is
Six standing functions, their charters (the mandates table), the leader's board of programs and each
member's cos-board, the seat-name form, attendance and its modes, the Operating Guide. It defines the
*shape of the seats*, not any one seat and not the sessions that hold them.

## Dialectic (thesis, antithesis, synthesis per name)

### `org`
- Thesis: the Rules already call it "the organization craft" (Rule-3 ×2, Rule-16 ×2) and the craft's own
  vocabulary files every term under `organization`; `org` is that word in the short form crafts already use
  (`sysarch`, `syseng`). The preference it reads is `organization`. Names the whole, overlaps no seat and
  no role term, collides with nothing in the client systems (their crafts: cos, arch, okrplan, pdm, sysimpl,
  harvester, translate, www).
- Antithesis: generic — "org" says *that* there is a structure, not which; every future structural craft
  (a flat variant, a multi-Operator tree) would want the same word, and `org` in industry usage is the
  *company* (org chart, org-wide), larger than a dyad system.
- Synthesis: the generic reading is the right one here — the craft is the one place the system's structure
  lives, and the preference key `organization: flat | m4` already makes "org" the axis that selects among
  structures, so a second structural craft would be a value of this one, not a sibling.

### `fleet`
- Thesis: the Operator's framing in #412 — a hundred sessions, one attended, the program orchestrating
  across them — is a fleet picture: many agents, one controller, span by attendance. Industry usage fits
  that picture exactly (fleet management of devices, vehicles, robots, agents).
- Antithesis: a fleet is a set of *homogeneous* units steered from outside; the craft's content is the
  opposite — six *differentiated* functions, charters that scope authority by content, boards, attendance,
  and a tree, not a flat set under a controller. "Fleet" names the sessions (what #412 said does not
  matter) and not the seats, charters and boards (what the craft defines). It also names no human party:
  a fleet has an operator but no Operator *in* it; the dyad's Operator sits at seats.
- Synthesis: `fleet` fits the *mechanism of attendance over many sessions* — one chapter of the Guide —
  and misnames the rest. If a craft for session orchestration were ever split out (spawning, routing,
  the relay), `fleet` is its name; it is not this craft's.

### `cabinet`
- Thesis: the Operator's own word from the start ("member-cabinet", "the other seats of the cabinet",
  "ds-cabinet-term-1"): the leader's cabinet of functions is the picture of a chief of staff with
  counsel, audit, engineer, distribute and operate — a government cabinet, exactly the M4 tree one level
  down. Concrete, evocative, already in the craft's vocabulary.
- Antithesis: `cabinet` is *already a defined term inside the craft* with a narrower meaning —
  `member-cabinet`: a seat on a member's board holding one topic (the `<topic>` slot; the client and asg
  cabinets). Naming the craft after one of its own leaf terms repeats the exact fault #415 found in
  `cos` (a craft named after a role it defines) and in `program` (named after one seat). And the
  Operator just said the cabinet is "the other seats", i.e. the members minus program — the craft also
  defines program.
- Synthesis: `cabinet` is the right name for what a member keeps under it, and the craft's vocabulary
  already uses it that way; promoting it to the craft name would need `member-cabinet` renamed first.
  Not worth the second collision.

## Survivor (Rule-10)
Path: `org`. It is the name the Rules already use, names the whole and nothing narrower, collides with no
seat, role, client craft or existing term. Counter: `cabinet` is the Operator's own word and the most
concrete of the three. Reconciliation: `cabinet` keeps its narrower, already-defined place inside the
craft (`member-cabinet`); `fleet` is reserved for a session-orchestration craft if one is ever split out.
Rename row (not opened here; engineer): `git mv crafts/cos crafts/org`, MANIFEST `name: org`, VERSION
0.2.0 (fresh tag line `org-v0.2.0`; `cos-v0.1.2` stays as history, its archive never published),
in-tree mentions; agent PR for Rule-3, Rule-16 and vocabulary paths; infra PR for the BUNDLE row;
the PREFERENCES line is the Operator's PR. Three zones, referent first (Rule-1).
