# Falsification record — "M4 is the default organization every dyad system runs under" (`../../rules/organization.md`; dyad-system #383, #369 C9)

**Claim (Operator's, provenance #369 entry 1 and #368):** M4 — a tree of operator-agent pairs, each a cos with a
cos-board, authority granted by content-scoped mandates, every pair sharing the same ledgers — is codified as the
default organizational preference, with an Operating Guide for Operators. The nine attacks on whether a `cos`
craft overlaps another craft's domain are plan #369 revision 4's (survivor: three cuts — Rule-8/sysadmin's,
Rule-19's, syseng's); the attacks below are on the rule's content, run while writing it against the first live
program (#369) rather than against the plan.

| # | Attack | Result | Survivor |
|---|--------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **The rule binds the Agent's process** (Rule-4 Boundaries: a rule that binds the process is an Agent Rule and belongs in the core; `check_tended` warns on the tokens). | **Refuted at drafting; the classification stays inference** | The craft guard's agent-token scan reports no token after drafting (`the Agent`, `counter-prompt`, `Y/N:`, `ratif`, `plan-Y`, `Done-Y` absent from `rules/*.md`); what a row is, who disposes, what a mandate binds and what may never be accreted are cited to Rules 2, 3, 7, 15, 16 and never restated — the Rule-8/sysadmin cut (#369 revision 4, attack 1). What the rule binds is the Operator's practice of the tree; its §5 conduct is what the Operator tends, and sysadmin's rules trip the same inference (#88). |
| 2 | **A one-pair system is forced to install the craft** (the default applies to every system). | **Refuted** | §1's first bullet and the Guide's §6: a one-pair system runs M4 already — its seat is the leader, its ledger the board, the core's Rules its charter — and the core waits for nothing (Rule-16 warns once about the form and checks nothing without the craft). The craft is installed at the second seat. |
| 3 | **"The same ledgers" is unenforceable: nothing checks that two pairs read one store.** | **Survives, scoped** | It is a precondition, not a check: a seat whose ledger another cannot read is not in the tree, and its work is an intake (§2). What the core does check is that every seat's state is in the one store — the row fence, the provenance count, the presence guard — so a pair outside the store is visible as absence, never as a false row. Stated as inference. |
| 4 | **A charter is identity-scoped the moment it names a seat** (its `seat` column). | **Refuted** | The `seat` column keys the row; the fence (paths, kinds of act) is what the charter grants, and a holder is never checked (#253 R24; `seat-vs-harness-session.md`). A charter whose fence is "whatever counsel does" would be identity-scoped and is not one the template writes. |
| 5 | **Six functions is a component cut in disguise** (#368 revision 3: a seat owning everyone's tests but no product is the anti-pattern). | **Refuted with the seams written in** | §4's table carries the seam per function — engineer the machine, distribute its shipping, the seam `package.py` already draws (#369 revision 8, attack 1) — and audit delegates and accretes nothing (Three Lines, attack 2 there). Without the seam column the attack stands; the column is the survivor. |
| 6 | **The leader's "never" list is longer than the core's never-accretable list, so a charter could widen it back.** | **Refuted** | §3 puts the core's list under every charter and says charters narrow; §5's list adds only conduct the core does not fence (touching another seat's presence file, opening a fourth seat), which the Operator revokes by the table. Nothing widens by a row. |
| 7 | **The program's own run contradicts the rule somewhere.** | **Confirmed once, carried into the Guide** | The leader held three seats idle on a sequential chain (revision 5's own counter) and did so for the organization's sake; the Guide's §7 prices it rather than the rule hiding it. No other act of #369 fell outside §5: no seat merged, released, wrote a preference, touched another's presence file or acted on the host. |
| 8 | Easy agreement (Rule-9 Conditions): the Operator said M4 and the plan said codify it. | **Pushed to 1, 3 and 7** | 1 moved the conduct's wording; 3 scoped a precondition to inference; 7 put the one contradiction into the Guide. |

**Verdict:** the claim **survives, scoped** — as practice the Operator tends (1), applying to a one-pair system by
doing nothing (2), with the same-ledgers precondition stated and not fenced (3), the charters content-scoped by
construction (4) and the functions cut by seam (5).

**Survivor:** `../../rules/organization.md` as written; the template `../../templates/mandates.md` with the six default
charters; the Guide's §7 as the record of the first run's cost.

**Limits.** The rule is read against one program (#369) on one system; the enterprise case (several Operators,
several repositories) is stated from #366's stress test and not yet lived. Whether a roll-up line was honest, and
whether a seat stayed inside its fence, is inference the Operator does from the rows.

Disposition: see ledger #383 (accreted under #369; its Done rides that mandate's Done disposition).
